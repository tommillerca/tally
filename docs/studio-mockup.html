The Studio: sharing your Bonehead
Boneheadz · sharing · plan only, nothing built

The Studio

Nobody shares a screenshot of a food log. They share a character they built. Right now there is no way to get a picture of your Bonehead out of this app at all, which means every hour a player spends dressing one up dies inside the app. This is the feature that lets it out.

The thesis, and it decides the whole shape: the feature is a picture worth sharing, with someone you want to share it with. A DM to one friend and a Story are equally good outcomes. The full Studio ships first as its own screen. Auto-cards follow, using the same composer.

The seating maths, because this is where mockups usually cheat

Every figure is placed by its ink, never its box. Most catalogue art sits in a 640px square with a lot of transparent air, so two characters with identical boxes stand at different heights. The renderer takes the fully composed character's alpha bounding box and ground footprint, scales so the drawing is a given height, seats the footprint on the ground line, and puts the shadow under the footprint's centre rather than the box's. Compose body layers at their shared registration before trimming; never trim garments separately. The standalone pet is then clamped: it shrinks until it fits inside the card with a real margin, because a companion sliced by the frame edge is worse than a smaller one. That is the same arithmetic the real compositor has to do, which is why it is in the mockup.

Cards with your buddy in them

Tom, 2026-08-09: "what about also the ability to create a screenshot with your buddy's bonehead?" Two characters can make a picture feel like a shared joke. Sending it to the friend in it is as much the point as putting it on a Story; two characters do not promise two posts.

The appearance data is already there

socialSnapshot carries outfit and pet appearance, including shiny, lineage and morph. Pet clothing is carried separately in yard.wear; a renderer must assemble the appearance input deliberately. Pass only the look and permitted display names into the card, never the full friend profile. Pet level may inform its appearance internally; it must not become a printed stat.

The trap is still foreign state. Never pass another player's pet through S.shinyPets: that collection is yours. Resolve their shiny, morph, clothing, tint and effects from their own appearance input. Add direct compositor coverage; the existing tests/figure-audit.mjs scans js/app.js, so a SITES row alone will not guard a separate canvas renderer.

Characters yes. Names opt-in.

Tom's ruling, 2026-09-09: an unnamed character is not an identity export for this feature. A friend's character may appear freely. No per-card approval flow or server export-consent service is needed.

  • Crew characters. Pick from your friends, not the public leaderboard. Each friend's character and pet can appear without asking them to approve a card.
  • Naming is their switch. Settings: "Let crew name me on their cards", off by default. Only an explicit on from that friend permits their display name. The exporter cannot switch it on for them. If current naming permission is unavailable, omit the name and keep the character.
  • Their friend code NEVER. Exclude it from the card input entirely. Only the exporter's code may appear, as plain text after they enable the visible preview toggle. No QR in v1.
  • VERSUS shows an unnamed loser. This overrides naming opt-in, including when the exporter lost. Suppress the loser's identifying labels and the exporter's code if it would identify that loser.
  • No stats or health-derived text. No levels, streaks, health numbers or health-context captions on any card, including frames and stickers. Names are the controlled display-name field, not editable card copy.

Two things that will bite

  • Saved looks and names are different. Refresh the appearance and naming preference before export. If the fetch fails, the preview says "Saved look" and omits the friend's name. Persisting the naming preference still needs ordinary profile wiring; the per-image approval and server authorization system proposed in the reviews is out of scope. Turning naming off affects future app exports, not already saved pictures.
  • Mirroring flips lettering. Keep both figures facing the same way and let the VS badge carry the confrontation, as in the mockup. Nose-to-nose would need per-item safe-to-flip information and is not required.

The Studio first, auto-cards next

1. The Studio: the deliberate one

Its own full screen, reached from the Wardrobe and from your own Crew profile. This ships first. Your Bonehead stands on a set. You choose the backdrop, whether the pet is in shot, the frame, a sticker or two, and whether it talks. Then you choose DM or Story and export. It is the flex, and it should feel like arranging a shot rather than ticking boxes: tap a backdrop and the set changes behind them immediately, no preview button.

2. Auto-cards: after the Studio ships

A later step reuses the Studio composer inside existing celebrations, with one extra button: Share this. It opens the same preview, code toggle and DM / Story choice. A private send counts just as much as a public Story. Internal moments can trigger an offer without printing their health or level data.

Frames: the North Face meets the video store

My first pass called a frame "a border and a corner mark", which is the safe answer and a boring one. Tom: "uninspired ... it's a spooky fun street style game ... think Nike and the North Face meets horror movies." Right. A frame should not decorate the picture, it should costume it: technical sportswear graphics language crossed with the horror shelf of a video shop. Rendered, not described.

Six frames, six different Boneheadz, all 1080×1920 August references. Three families: street (Bib, Hangtag), horror (Rental, Tracking, One-sheet) and foil (Foil). Every one of them is CSS and SVG over the same composite. No new art from Cam is required for a single one, which is the point: this is a whole cosmetic line that costs illustration time only when we want it to.

Why these make people want in, rather than just looking nice

  • The character carries the story. BIB uses a fixed decorative number, not a level. Frames never print health-derived text or stats. A purchased frame says nothing about what the player achieved.
  • Frames are earned AND purchasable. Either route unlocks the cosmetic, including FOIL. Ownership allows selection and export. Any optional game-event stamp must still be true of the depicted look; no streak frame or health-derived annotation in v1.
  • They rotate. Frames ride the drop calendar like every other cosmetic. Seasonal ones (a Halloween Rental variant) are not there forever, so the cards themselves get datestamped by which frame they wear.
  • The character always wins. Every treatment above was pulled back once because atmosphere was eating the Bonehead. A horror frame that hides the thing you spent a month dressing is a failed frame.

Two technical findings worth deciding on now

The actual art and the 1080 card

The grill's census of 378 catalogue files found 372 at 640×640 and six at 2048×2048, with none missing. All 22 backdrops are 640×640. That census covers primary catalogue files, not every mask, morph, shiny variant or thumbnail.

There is no 1000px master library. The proposed master-fetch and 13MB download plan is removed. Render from the real catalogue files, keep text and frame graphics crisp at export size, and judge source-art softness on device. Taking a full 640px source to 1080px is about a 1.7× upscale; alpha-trimmed art can need more. The six larger files do not make the whole catalogue high resolution.

Use cached required assets offline. If a required asset is missing, keep the draft and explain what needs downloading, rather than exporting an incomplete character. Sharper replacement art would be a separately scoped asset job, not an assumed dependency or a hidden fetch.

Sharing an image file is the real unknown

The two existing share calls in the app pass title and text. Putting a file through navigator.share from inside a Capacitor WKWebView is still uncertain, there is no @capacitor/share in the project, and I will not assume it works. This gets tested on a real device before anything else is built, because the answer changes the plan:

  • If file sharing works: choose DM or Story in the preview, then use the tested handoff. A system sheet may still require choosing the destination; do not promise direct sending before device proof.
  • If it does not: add the Capacitor share plugin, or save to Photos and let the player attach it to a DM or Story from there. Both are fine, but they are different buttons and different copy.
  • On the web build, test a download link and image-save fallback in the supported browsers.

The renderer still needs font, decode, encoding, memory and cancellation proof in the real app runtime. Desktop mockups do not establish WKWebView parity or a millisecond budget. A successful share-sheet handoff is not proof that a DM was sent or a Story was posted.

How it gets built

The reviews favoured a smaller sharing trial before the full editor; Tom ruled that the full Studio screen ships first.

  1. Answer the transport question on a real device A technical prerequisite, not an auto-card release. Try a representative 1080×1920 image through the real iOS build into both DM and Story, including cancellation and the save fallback. each route reaches its destination preview or has honest, tested manual attachment steps.
  2. The compositor and its guards Look plus allowed options in, a 1080×1920 Blob out.   3. The full Studio screen, first shipping surface Its own screen, entered from Wardrobe and Crew. Complete the preview controls before release. code starts off on every route; opting in changes the preview and exported pixels; each friend's name needs their naming opt-in; VERSUS loser stays unnamed; export matches preview. Cached complete looks export offline, missing assets preserve the draft, and cancellation returns to it.
  4. Frames and stickers, part of that Studio release Wire earned and purchased ownership into the picker. Both routes unlock frames; approved text and decoration follow the same privacy rules. Persist the friend's naming preference without per-card approval plumbing. earned and purchased frames both select and export; unowned frames cannot be forced into an export; a cosmetic purchase cannot create a false shiny stamp. Missing naming permission omits the name.
  5. Auto-cards, after the full Studio ships Six internal moments, one offer per session, dismissal remembered, always inside an existing celebration. Open the same preview with the code off and DM / Story choices; no level or streak text on the resulting card. all six in one session produce exactly one offer; dismissal survives reload; the same privacy and export guards pass before this later release.

Risks

  • A send leaves the app. DM and Story both create copies the app cannot retract. The code toggle is visible and off by default; a friend's name needs their opt-in, their code never leaves, and the VERSUS loser stays unnamed. Missing naming permission means no name, not a blocked character card.
  • Preview trust. No destination may silently add a code, stats or health-derived copy. Saved August sample art is not the v1 export contract. The old poster credits and identifying labels remain only in those frozen references.
  • Resolution and transport still need proof. Most sources are 640px. Verify the export and each DM / Story handoff on device; keep the draft through failures. Visual review of this revised page is owed.
  • Naming is done. Zero placeholder rows remain from the 258-name job; all 22 backdrops have real names. Readability review remains, not a rename blocker.
  • Scope. The full Studio is the first release, including its composer controls and earned/purchased frames. Auto-cards follow. The shipping feature does not need camera access.
