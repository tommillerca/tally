## The two long-running mysteries, both answered

**"The hype banners never moved on the live app."** They always moved and still
do. Measured on live: 29 to 50% of pixels changing with motion allowed, and
**exactly 0.000%** under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Two mechanisms fire together:
app.css kills every animation globally under reduce, and the drop reel's JS
driver is skipped entirely so it rests on its first beat forever, which is
literally a static grid. **Tom's phone almost certainly has Reduce Motion on.**
The iOS Simulator ships with it off, which is why he suddenly sees motion there.
No mockup ever promised motion for these banners.

**"The overscroll wordmark never worked."** It never could. `viewport-fit=cover`
plus `black-translucent` puts viewport y=0 UNDER the status bar, and `bottom:
100%` pins the mark's bottom edge to y=0, so it lives entirely inside
`env(safe-area-inset-top)`. On a Dynamic Island phone that is exactly where a
centred 232px wordmark rides. The app's own fade calls 36px of pull "FULL
opacity", a distance at which 100% of the mark is behind the status bar.

The guard's gap, named: `REACHABLE` computes the pull at which the mark crosses
**y = 0, the scrollport origin**, not the first VISIBLE row, and prints "needs
0px of rubber-band travel" on every inset. Its partner row exists specifically
to keep `--sat` out of the threshold, so the one term that decides occlusion was
forbidden by construction.

**The fix was deliberately NOT shipped**, and that is the right call: the bounce
itself cannot be reproduced on this machine (Chromium clamps scrollTop, and a
synthesised drag in the iOS Simulator moved Today zero pixels, as did a bare
control page, so the touch is the limitation). **One check on a real iPhone
settles it: pull down on Today and watch whether the Bonehead card moves at
all.** If it does not, the nested scroller does not rubber-band in WKWebView and
the whole mechanism is dead regardless of geometry.
