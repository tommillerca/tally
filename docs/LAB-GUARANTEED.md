# Laboratory pace with guaranteed Midnight

Historical protected baseline. The current unprotected ruling and paired measurements are in [LAB-RISK.md](LAB-RISK.md). The executable appendix remains historical comparison evidence.

**The guarantee removes the final-recipe failure tail, but right-species Base supply still binds.** At one slot, daily full completion is **192 / 223 days** and casual is **703 / 817** (median / P90). Incubators remain weak collection accelerators for these egg-fed starting profiles. Daily first colour passes the two-week target. Casual first colour still misses one month, and daily first complete species takes about four months rather than three. Full 36-cell completion is a long-term chase, not a failed short-term target.

## Ruling and interpretation boundary

| Same-species pair, both consumed | Output before the existing lower-tier protection |
| --- | --- |
| Base + Base | Ember 50% / Frost 50% |
| Ember + Frost | Toxic 50% / Rose 50% |
| Toxic + Rose | Midnight 100% |

Every result is an immediate fresh level-1 pet, with zero banked training and lineage, not an egg or an incubation. The final recipe has one supported result, no Toxic return, no outcome draw and no fake gamble animation. The lower tiers retain the existing species-local collection-first, then 2/2 ingredient protection, including their legitimate 100% cases. Same-species eligibility, consume-both, trained-pet warnings, shiny/CX exclusion, six colours, 36 cells, unchanged Base/shiny eggs and faucets, the free daily use, purchasable slots and all three sinks remain locked.

**Literal wording conflict:** “every experiment returns something the player could not have before” cannot mean a never-owned collection colour on every use while keeping duplicate ingredient production and the spec's post-completion copy making. Even the safe route to one Midnight must make duplicate Ember/Frost and Toxic/Rose feedstock. Last-copy consumption can also reduce current cell count. The proposed interpretation for review is that each recipe advances beyond its input bracket, with two distinct possible colours only where the bracket contains two colours. Collection/stock protection and duplicate outputs remain legal. These measurements use the explicit recipe table and otherwise unchanged prior method; they do not certify a literal always-new-cell promise or silently add eligibility restrictions. Requiring literal novelty instead would need a new ruling on those conflicting locked requirements.

## Comparable milestones

All entries are calendar days, **median / P90**, at one slot. Old figures are the consume-both measurements in PRIOR-PACE, not the earlier V4 retained-parent forecast.

| Profile | Milestone | Old final gamble | Guaranteed Midnight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Casual, 1.6 eggs/week | First colour | 43 / 47 | 43 / 47 |
| Casual | First complete species | 497 / 610 | 423 / 470 |
| Casual | All 36 cells | 1,240 / 1,844 | 703 / 817 |
| Daily, 5.9 eggs/week | First colour | 12 / 13 | 12 / 13 |
| Daily | First complete species | 136 / 168 | 116 / 130 |
| Daily | All 36 cells | 338 / 502 | 192 / 223 |

“Complete” means **currently retaining all six cells** of a species, or all 36 cells across C1-C6. It does not count colours that were discovered and then consumed. “First species” is whichever completes first. A prespecified C1 completes at 549 / 698 casual and 151 / 191 daily, compared with 742 / 1,281 and 203 / 349 before. A favourite species is therefore a slower objective than the first of any species.

The collection-preserving policy leaves a Base keeper before the first colour experiment. The earlier first eligible ordinary Base pair remains 29 / 29 days casual and 9 / 9 daily. Those are eligibility observations, not first-colour measurements under a second strategy. Manually spending the last two Bases is legal with warnings, but changing to that strategy would break the requested direct comparison and may delay current-cell completion.

Full completion improves by about 43% at the median and 56% at P90 in both profiles. The new full-completion P99 is 934 days casual and 255 daily, with sampled maxima 1,345 and 367. These maxima are not guarantees. Final attempts are now bounded exactly, but random right-species egg arrivals still have no universal finite calendar bound.

## Cost of one Midnight

The final action itself is exactly **one experiment, two input pets destroyed, one level-1 Midnight returned**. It used to average 3.5 final attempts and seven final-input identities destroyed. Rebuilding ingredients was an additional cost, not included in that seven.

For direct comparison with PRIOR-PACE, the following includes all lower-tier construction while preserving the complete six-cell species:

| Cost per completed species, including one Midnight | Old theoretical mean, E[K] = 3.5 | Guaranteed, K = 1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Final experiments | 3.5 | 1 |
| Final-input pet identities consumed | 7 | 2 |
| All experiments, including feedstock construction | 25 | 15 |
| Ordinary Base feedstock consumed | 30 | 20 |
| Matched Base allocation, including one retained keeper | 31 | 21 |
| Pet identities consumed across all recipes | 50 | 30 |

The guaranteed construction is ten Base + Base experiments (20 ordinary Bases), four Ember + Frost experiments (eight intermediate inputs), and one Toxic + Rose experiment (two intermediate inputs). The 15 outputs minus 30 consumed identities leave a net loss of 15 pets; starting with 21 matched Bases leaves the six owned cells. A shiny Base can substitute for the keeper only. Identity destruction includes intermediates made and consumed along the way; it is not an extra hatch charge.

The old formulas were experiments `11 + 4K` and matched allocation `17 + 4K`. Setting K to one eliminates an average 2.5 failures, ten experiments and ten ordinary Bases per species. All six species now take exactly **90 experiments, 120 ordinary Base feedstock plus six keepers, and 180 consumed identities**, versus theoretical means of 150 experiments, 180 feedstock plus six keepers, and 300 consumed identities before.

These collection costs are not the minimum cost of obtaining only a Midnight while sacrificing all other cells. That separate construction is eight matched Bases, four lower recipes, two middle recipes and one final recipe: seven experiments and 14 consumed identities, leaving only Midnight. It is not the strategy used in any pace table.

Extra C1 Bases cannot pay for missing C6 Bases.

## Is the pace acceptable?

| Requested experience | Measurement | Judgment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily first colour inside two weeks | 12 / 13 days | Passes at median and P90 under the main scheduling assumptions. Weekly batching gives 15 / 15, so the claim is not robust to all equally averaged grant schedules. |
| Casual first colour inside a month | 43 / 47 days | Misses even a 31-day month at both median and P90. The final-tier change cannot repair this early milestone. |
| Daily first complete species inside a few months | 116 / 130 days, roughly 3.8 / 4.3 months | Still slow. Using about three months as the review benchmark, both miss. If “a few” permits four months, the median is around that boundary and P90 still exceeds it. No exact month limit was specified. |
| All 36 cells as a long-term chase | Daily 192 / 223; casual 703 / 817 days | Compatible with a long-term objective. No maximum acceptable long-term duration was supplied, so this is not independently certified as enjoyable. |

**Overall: improved, but not fully acceptable against the stated early milestones.** Do not bring back the previous V4 completion targets as acceptance gates. The clear unresolved miss is casual first colour; daily first species remains a roughly four-month milestone. No legal capacity-only change fixes the casual first colour result, since even three slots leave it unchanged. This order authorizes reporting the miss, not changing the faucet, adding a starter grant, weakening consume-both or silently changing the collection policy.

## Method, checks and limitations

The executable appendix is adapted directly from PRIOR-PACE's appendix. **All 13 scenarios run 20,000 trials each**, indices 0 through 19,999: two profiles times three slot counts, three daily ample-stock scenarios, two weekly-batch scenarios and two save-for-slots scenarios. The same 20,000 player seeds are reused across scenarios; 260,000 scenario runs are not independent players. The old appendix is also rerun unchanged as a comparison control.

The deterministic generator is Mulberry32. Hatch seeds are `20260908 + imul(index,2654435761)`. Lower-recipe seeds per species are `1234567 + imul(index,2246822519) + imul(speciesIndex+1,1013904223)`. The old final streams used `7654321 + imul(index,3266489917) + imul(speciesIndex+1,374761393)`; they are removed because the final outcome is certain. The hatch and lower stream definitions are otherwise unchanged. To preserve exact prior seed advancement, lower singleton recipes still advance their measurement stream; that draw cannot affect their certain result and is not a prescription for production RNG. Quantiles use nearest rank, zero-based `ceil(N*q)-1`.

The inherited assumptions are explicit:

- Start with no cells and open the one existing ready welcome egg for day one. No starter grants, purchased pets, legacy colours or starting hoard in the main runs. No competing salvage/breeding or reservation of trained pets is simulated.
- Day one is Monday. Casual visits Monday/Wednesday/Friday/Sunday with 5,000 effective incubation steps per visit. Daily visits every day with 15,500 effective steps per visit. These are the prior model's assumptions, not observed player activity.
- End-of-day recurring grants are `floor(day*rate/7)-floor((day-1)*rate/7)`, at the frozen 1.6/5.9 weekly rates. Their anchors exclude steps already accumulated that day. Eggs incubate concurrently to the real 8,000-step goal and hatch on visits. Casual needs two subsequent visits and daily one. A Laboratory output is immediately usable within remaining daily capacity and gets no incubation delay.
- Recipe selection preserves every current cell, prefers direct missing outputs before ingredient stock preparation, and breaks ties C1 through C6. Base consumes two ordinary copies while leaving a Base or shiny Base keeper. Middle/final consume one of each input from at least two eligible copies apiece. These are strategy choices, not restrictions on manual trained/last-copy eligibility. Completed species are not rerolled.
- The same real `pickRandomPet`, `eggProgress`, `ownedPairs`, `SHINY_CHANCE`, `SHINY_ART` and `EGG_GOAL_STEPS` are imported locally. The unconditional shiny draw precedes species selection and the art gate. Kept species agree with species ownership used by the real helper.
- The 100,000-day guard throws on an incomplete trial; no guard date is recorded as completion and no incomplete trial is censored. All reported trials complete. Fixed average faucets alone cannot identify unique real-player percentiles; actual arrival variance, inactivity, different strategies and competing sinks can change the results.

**Inherited helper incompatibility:** shipped `rollMorph` has five morphs, no Rose, cross-species freshness and a global weighted fallback. It cannot literally resolve this six-colour recipe graph unchanged. The disclosed measurement deviation remains a local recipe adapter; the real `rollMorph` is exercised only by the 1,000-draw legacy freshness control. No shipped helper, eligibility rule, faucet or runtime recipe was modified. This report does not satisfy LAB-23's future requirement to test an actual implemented Laboratory resolver.

Every simulated action asserts eligible non-shiny inputs, net minus one pet and preservation of current cells. Every completed species asserts exactly one final attempt, 15 experiments and 20 consumed ordinary Bases. Full-grid checks assert six final attempts, 90 experiments and inventory conservation. The ordered lower-protection control is retained, paired capacity scenarios retain identical per-species attempt vectors, and all ample-stock trials assert the exact `ceil(90/slots)` completion floor. Old geometric distribution checks are retained only in the unchanged old-model control; they are replaced by deterministic cost assertions in the guaranteed model. These are numerical checks, not production transaction, UI or animation proof.
