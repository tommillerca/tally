## Design Specificity Verdict

Specific in voice, generic in composition. The keeper's lines cannot drift out of sync with the board because hlwLine() is fed from the same gardenState() the beds render from, in the same pass. That is architecture, not writing. But the layout (arch sign, shed top-right, path down the middle, bed grid, price tag on the locked plot, walking helper) is a stock farm screen once the skeleton is cropped out.

Two omissions cause the loss: the screen never states that harvests feed the cauldrons (the list version's copy does), and --accent (#a5e847) appears exactly once, in a 2.4s toast, on an Operate surface whose job is showing where to go. The designer's first-visit comp had a lime arrow at the shed; the build dropped it along with "FREE STARTER SEEDS" and the bottom instruction bar.

Deterministic scan: 6 findings on js/app.js (broken-image x6 at lines 240, 7317, 8287, 9728, 12381, 15854) and 1 on index.html (single-font). ALL FALSE POSITIVES: every broken-image hit is the literal string `<img>` inside a JS comment; single-font misses Bangers because it arrives via var(--display) in CSS. Zero true findings on the Hollow surface.

## What's Working

1. The keeper cannot lie, structurally. hlwLine() takes {ripe, growing, planted, owned, seeds, firstEver, daysAway} derived from the same garden.plots array that renders the beds. Most character-voice systems drift; this one cannot, by construction.
2. The keeper stands on grass, not on a .bh-stage card. Two first-time reviewers had assumed the card was a failed image load. Removing it is why the scene reads as a place rather than a widget.
3. The frame does real grouping. Ghost slots inside it read as "yours, later"; the identical dashed ellipse outside it read as nothing. hlwBuySpot derives from HLW_SPOTS so the sign cannot drift off the slot.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Reduced motion does not reduce motion, app-wide.**
app.css:503 sets `animation-duration: 0.001s !important` globally. That does not remove animation, it runs it ~1000 cycles/second. 9 of 10 animations in the Hollow still report playState `running` under emulated reduce, all with infinite iterations, so each painted frame samples a different phase. Measured: reduced motion cut frame-to-frame pixel change by 26% at 390x844 and 2% at 375x667, and fireflies changed MORE pixels with reduce ON (3,388 vs 3,120). The project rule is that reduced motion removes effects rather than freezing them mid-frame. This affects every screen, not just the Hollow.
Fix: replace the duration override with `animation: none !important` (or `animation-iteration-count: 1` plus `animation-fill-mode: forwards`) for infinite decorative loops, and keep finite one-shots collapsing to their end state.
Suggested command: /impeccable audit

**[P1] Adjacent bed hit boxes overlap 21px, and the overlap spends coins.**
HLW_SPOTS places row-mates 63px apart while .hlw-bed is 84px wide at cx-42. Confirmed by elementFromPoint: bed0's top-right and bottom-right return bed1; bed2's top-right and bottom-right return #hlwBuy, the coin-spend control, because #hlwBuy is appended after the bed buttons and all share z-index 6. Each bed loses 25% of its width. Measured intersection 20x58 css px at 390, 19x55 at 375.
Fix: .hlw-bed to 60x56 positioned at cx-30 (3px gutter, still ~55x51 device px, above the 40px floor). Add a per-bed hit-test assertion at centre and both thirds.
Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P1] The bed that needs water has no cue, and the droplet means something else.**
.hlw-chip renders a blue droplet plus fmtCookTime for every bed matching `!p.empty && !p.ready`, so a fully watered bed still wears a droplet. The bed that actually wants water (p.canWater) is signalled only by a leaf fill shifting #7fae57 to #9fae6a, roughly a 3% hue change. The list version has a .thirsty class and a loud drop badge; the diorama lost it. Watering is the only mid-loop action and the only thing that changes yield.
Fix: drop the droplet from the timer chip (clock glyph or bare time). Reserve the droplet for p.canWater and give that bed the screen's single accent moment: --accent ring plus a NEEDS WATER chip. That spends the brand's "go" colour exactly where the brand says to spend it.
Suggested command: /impeccable colorize

**[P1] The only instruction on the screen is below the fold.**
Measured: #hollowBody scrolls 176px at 390x844 and 318px at 375x667. The `.note` carrying the loop, the water rule and "Nothing ever dies" is below the fold at both sizes. At 375x667 the compost heap (#hlwCrow) and the keeper are entirely below the fold too; all five hit probes return OFFSCREEN until scrolled.
Fix: shorten the stage from 900 to 780 units (deleting only empty path), delete the sheet header and use a 44px round back chevron over the diorama as the comp specifies (reclaims ~90px and removes the duplicate title), which is enough to bring the note above the fold.
Suggested command: /impeccable layout

**[P1] Taps are silently swallowed for up to 4.4s, and one bed state never responds.**
Every handler opens with `if (busy) return`. busy spans the walk (0.9-2.4s) plus 2s pour or 1.2s pluck, with nothing on screen indicating it. Separately a bed that is growing and already watered walks the keeper over and then sets busy=false with no toast, no line change, no sound.
Fix: on busy, set pointer-events:none on #hlwStage and dim non-target beds so the block is visible. On the growing-and-watered branch, fire toastAt with the remaining time. Cap av.dur at 1.2s.
Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P2] The buy confirmation is unstyled text over the garden, and affordability arrives too late.**
armToConfirm sets btn.innerHTML and adds .arming, but no `.hlw-bed.arming` rule exists in app.css. .hlw-bed is background:none/border:none/padding:0, so "Spend 1,500?" renders as bare cream body text wrapping inside an 84x60 transparent box over the ghost slot and sign post. Affordability (coins < price) is only checked after the second tap, with 340 coins against a 1,500 sign.
Fix: add a .hlw-bed.arming rule (ink ground, accent border and text, display face). Gate earlier: at coin < bedPrice render the sign at 55% opacity with a NEED N MORE chip and skip arming.
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P2] .sheet-close is 43.8 x 30.9 px, under the project's own 40px minimum, and is the only exit on the screen.**
app.css:241, padding 6px 4px, no min-height.
Suggested command: /impeccable audit

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer, literal, abandons rather than figures it out)**
- The keeper's opening line is a joke, not an instruction: "So this is the Hollow. I am the one who stands here." No next step.
- The designer's first-visit comp solved this with a lime arrow at the shed, a FREE STARTER SEEDS label and a bottom bar reading "TAP THE SHED, YOUR STARTER SEEDS ARE INSIDE". None of those three exist in the build. Largest gap between intent and shipped.
- The seed shed is a trap: tapping it opens a read-only pouch list with "TAP TO CLOSE" and no plant button, so following the design's own guidance is a dead end.
- The unlabelled second ghost slot is a dashed void with no sign; the one above it has a price.
- The coin chip shows 340 and the sign shows 1,500, with no relationship drawn and no route to earning from here.

**Casey (thumb only, interrupted, low patience)**
- Up to 4.4s of blocked screen per tap, non-cancellable, no busy indicator, no queue.
- The overlapping hit boxes punish exactly the imprecise thumb tap she makes; a quarter of a bed's width belongs to its neighbour.
- The 30s setInterval rebuilds body.innerHTML wholesale; if she is mid-read it is replaced under her.
- toastAt lasts 2.4s at 12.5px; interrupted for three seconds she never learns what she harvested.
- At 375x667 the compost heap and keeper require a 318px scroll on a surface that reads as fixed.

**Robin (non-gamer who came to log food; derived from the product's "non-gamers must never be lost" constraint)**
- Every noun on screen is invented and none is defined here: Hollow, keeper, seed pouch, compost heap, ghost slot, bonehead.
- The screen never says what a harvest is FOR. The list version's "ready for the cauldrons" clause is absent, so she completes the loop and cannot say what she gained.
- There is no plain-language entry point, which the product doc names as a durable constraint for exactly this user. The keeper's dry voice is a great second register and a bad first one.
- The 1,500 sign against 340 coins reads as a paywall on a screen she did not ask to be on.

## Minor Observations

- firstEver self-destructs in ~30s: kvSet('hlwSeen') runs during the FIRST render, so the tick or any action flips it false and replaces the welcome line. Write it on sheet close instead.
- Five identical "Garden bed" aria-labels with no state. Include state: "Bed 3, tomato, ready to harvest" / "needs water" / "1h 12m left" / "empty". #hlwBuy should say the price.
- .hlw-say has no aria-live. It is the screen's primary status text.
- The price sign's top 12 stage px and the shed roof apex are drawn outside their own buttons: taps there hit bare SVG.
- The day band renders no tint at all, so the saturated #617442 lawn is the largest brightest object, in an app whose world is near-black grounds with quiet chrome.
- hlwBuySpot clamps with Math.min(owned, LEN-1), a latent overlap at owned===5 (currently unreachable because bedPrice is null at max).
- harvestPlot sets busy=false before setTimeout(render, 1200), so the 30s tick can wipe the pluck animation.
- Contrast passes everywhere: nine text elements measured against real composited pixels, lowest 5.00:1. Zero page errors across six runs.
- The speech bubble was suspected of eating controls and does not: pointer-events:none holds, and its rect intersects no control at either size.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the keeper cannot lie about the garden, why is he not the interface? He already knows every state. He could point, with gaze and position and a lime arrow, and his line names the thing. That collapses the hierarchy, thirst-cue and first-run failures into one mechanic only this product could have.
2. Is the walk a feature or a tax? It is both the biggest usability cost and the biggest charm. Paying it once on open, then resolving taps instantly, might keep 90% of the character for 10% of the wait.
3. Why do two full implementations of the same garden exist? The list has better error prevention, water cues and harvest payoff; the diorama has the soul. Every fix now has to be made twice or they drift.
4. What would this screen look like if the harvest, not the garden, were the subject? The biggest brightest element is currently real estate you do not own.
5. Does the Hollow know it is inside a calorie tracker? One line of copy re-attaching it to the cauldrons would stop it being a farming minigame.
